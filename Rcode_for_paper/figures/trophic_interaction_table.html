Ecological mechanisms governing Spring Chinook mortality, temperature-driven spatial overlap, and age-structured prey buffering across migration regions

Guild 1: Seabirds Common Murre, Cormorants, Gulls, Loons Smolt (Surface outmigration) High HCI contracts coastal upwelling zone; forces birds and smolts into narrow nearshore plume. High prey-switching: Abundant Anchovy, Market Squid, or Herring divert dive effort away from smolts. Hostetter et al. (2012);
Nur et al. (2011);
Phillips et al. (2017, 2021);
Tucker et al. (2016);
Zamon et al. (2014, 2024)
Guild 2: Demersal Fish & Mammals (NCC) Pacific Hake, Harbor Seals Smolt & Adult (Hake on smolts; Seals on adults) Warm SST/HCI drives Hake inshore/northward and concentrates seals near river mouths. Hake track Krill but switch to Anchovy/Squid; Seals switch to Eulachon/Hake. Emmett & Sampson (2007);
Olesiuk (1993);
Sabal et al. (2023);
Shirk et al. (2023);
Steingass (2017);
Thomas et al. (2017);
Wright et al. (2010)
Guild 3: Alaska Mammals & Small Piscivores Steller Sea Lion, Sablefish, Spiny Dogfish Smolt & Sub-Adult (Inshore to offshore shelf) SST shifts coastal forage aggregations; Sablefish and Sea Lions shift bathymetric depth with warming. SSLs & Sablefish track pelagic Age 1–2 Pollock cohorts and Herring in shallow shelf layers. Bredesen et al. (2004);
Coutré et al. (2015);
Daly et al. (2024);
Marston et al. (2002);
Sigler et al. (2009);
Sinclair & Zeppelin (2002);
Tollit et al. (2015)
Guild 4: Alaska Demersal Piscivores Arrowtooth Flounder, Pacific Halibut, Pacific Cod Adult (Deep benthic & demersal shelf) Warm GoA SST restricts benthic boundary layer; forces flatfish diel vertical migrations into midwater salmon zones. Piscivorous buffering: Large Arrowtooth & Halibut switch between benthic Age 1+ Pollock cohorts and pelagic Capelin. Bizzarro et al. (2023);
Knoth & Foy (2008);
Langan et al. (2024);
Wells et al. (2024, 2025);
Yang (1993, 2011)
Guild 5: Apex Pelagic Sharks Salmon Shark, Sleeper Shark Adult (Epipelagic & mesopelagic offshore) SST fronts in GoA/BSAI compress shark foraging depths directly into epipelagic adult salmon return corridors. Opportunistic apex generalists; target large Adult Pollock (Age 3+), Pink Salmon, and Herring during salmon runs. Carlisle et al. (2015);
Hulbert et al. (2005);
Nagasawa (1998);
Riddell et al. (2018);
